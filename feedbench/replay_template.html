Does capping same-community endorsements slow antisemitic spread?

We compare two feed-ranking rules in the same synthetic social network and measure antisemitic exposure and the cost to ordinary participation. The replay below shows saved, computed runs. It illustrates a mechanism under declared assumptions; it does not estimate effects on a real platform or establish a legal conclusion.

    Declared assumptions and limits

    • Agents are scripted, transparent behaviour rules, not a model of real people. An LLM action policy is a separate configuration, not used here.
    • Communities are assigned, not detected. Cluster size, placement and how strongly it endorses are scenario inputs.
    • Seeing antisemitic posts ends some sessions (15%) and makes a few agents leave (3%). This is an assumption, and it links exposure to participation.
    • Counterspeech replies are shown under the post they answer, so fewer antisemitic impressions also means fewer counterspeech replies seen.
    • Five seeds measure simulation variability only. They say nothing about how well the simulator represents humans.
    • Harmful texts are placeholders. The expert team replaces them with reviewed examples and records label rationales and disagreements.
